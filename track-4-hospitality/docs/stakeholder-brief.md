# Housekeeping and room-status board - what I need

From: Sofia Lindqvist, Director of Rooms, Larchmont Hotels
To: the team inheriting the reservations system
Date: 19 October 2026

I run rooms across all six hotels, and I will be honest: I am not technical. I want this fixed before the Christmas peak, and I would like to see something working in a few weeks, not months. Here is what I need, in the order it comes to me.

## Why I am asking

Last month, on three different days, the front desk at London Bridge sold and checked in a guest to a room that was still being cleaned. One guest walked in on the housekeeper. Another time a room that the engineers had pulled out of service for a leak was handed to a late arrival because the desk could not see it was off the market. Today housekeeping tells the desk by walkie-talkie and a whiteboard. When it goes wrong, I find out from a TripAdvisor review.

## What the board must do

Every room is in one of four states: Dirty, Cleaning, Inspected and Ready. When a guest checks out the room becomes Dirty. When a housekeeper starts it is Cleaning. When they finish, a supervisor checks it and it becomes Inspected, and then it is Ready and can be sold. Nothing goes to Ready without a supervisor inspection, no exceptions. I do not want a repeat of the leak room.

A room that is out of order is not for sale. Full stop. Maintenance should be able to put a room out of order with a reason, and it should come back through the same cleaning steps afterwards. The desk must never be able to assign a guest to an out-of-order room.

On a busy morning, though, the supervisors cannot be everywhere. Housekeepers should be able to mark a room Ready themselves when it is a simple turn, so the desk is not waiting on a supervisor who is on another floor.

## Turnaround time

I want a turnaround target of 45 minutes from checkout to Ready, for every room type, everywhere. Anything slower is a breach and should appear on a list I can look at each morning, worst first. In practice a suite takes a good hour and a family room is not much faster, so for those two types the target is 75 minutes. Breaches are what I will be judged on, so please make the clock honest. The clock should start when the guest leaves.

## Floors

Housekeepers work by floor, so the board should be organised by floor, and each housekeeper should be given a floor to look after so the work is fair. The busiest floors should be easy to see.

## What the front desk needs to see

For any room the desk wants one thing: when will this room be ready? Not a status, a time. They should be able to ask that for a single room and get the time, or be told that it is out of order and when it comes back.

## Two more things

1. When the guest's key card stops working because they have checked out, the lock system already knows. I want the board to turn the room Dirty the instant that happens, and to show on screen without anybody pressing refresh. That will save the desk a click on every checkout.
2. Housekeepers use their own phones for the staff app. I want the board to show where each housekeeper is on the floor plan, all the time, so I can send the nearest person. Some of them pick up extra shifts without clocking in, and I want to see them too, and during their breaks. If they do not like it, that is a conversation for HR.

## While you are in there

The Monday occupancy report looks wrong to me. It said Edinburgh was close to fifty percent last week and I know the building was nowhere near that. Our revenue manager is basing rate decisions on that number, so it matters. Please look at it.

## How I will judge it

Show me the board for one hotel, show me the late rooms list, and show me that the desk cannot sell a room that is not ready. I do not need pretty. I need it to be right.
